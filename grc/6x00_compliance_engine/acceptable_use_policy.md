# MedDefense Acceptable Use Policy

**Policy number:** SEC-AUP-001
**Effective date:** 2026-05-10
**Review date:** 2027-05-10 (Annual)
**Owner:** IT Security
**Approved by:** CISO

## Purpose
This policy protects MedDefense computers, networks, and patient data from hackers, misuse, and theft. It gives you clear and simple rules for using company technology so we can keep our patients safe and follow the law.

## Scope
This policy applies to every person with access to a MedDefense system. This strictly includes employees, contractors, vendors with remote access, and temporary clinical staff.

## Acceptable use
MedDefense computers, internet, and accounts are only for MedDefense business and patient care. You are not allowed to use these systems for personal reasons.

## Prohibited activities
The following activities are strictly prohibited and non-negotiable:
* Looking at patient records if you are not actively treating that patient.
* Sharing your passwords or letting anyone else use your account.
* Downloading or installing any software without asking IT.
* Connecting your personal phones or laptops to the clinical network without IT permission.
* Sending MedDefense data using your personal email, personal cloud storage, or private chat apps.
* Trying to open files, folders, or systems that are not part of your job.
* Putting patient data into AI tools (like ChatGPT), outside websites, or third-party apps without an approved Business Associate Agreement (a formal privacy contract signed by MedDefense).

## PHI handling obligations
You must only view, use, or share the absolute minimum amount of patient data (Protected Health Information, or PHI) needed to do your specific job. Looking at or sharing more patient data than you need is a direct violation of this policy.

## Remote access
If you work from outside the hospital, you must use our approved secure tools. You are never allowed to connect to MedDefense systems from public or shared Wi-Fi (like at a cafe) without turning on the official company VPN first.

## Reporting obligations
You must tell us if you see a suspicious email, strange computer behavior, lose a device, or accidentally share patient data. You must report these events immediately the moment you notice them by submitting an IT Security ticket and calling the IT Security phone line.

## Consequences of violation
If you break these rules, Human Resources will take disciplinary action, which can include firing you. Breaking these rules can also break HIPAA laws, meaning you could personally face heavy fines or criminal charges.

## Acknowledgment and review
All active personnel must review and sign this policy annually. Furthermore, new employees and contractors must sign this document at onboarding before system access is granted.
